--- layout: goal-group title: Thema 4 goal_number: '4' language: en permalink: /en/thema-4/ --- Within the framework developed by the Independent High-Level Expert Group, sustainability and resilience describe the extent to which a society is able to safeguard the foundations of its well-being and development over the long term, while remaining capable of responding effectively to changing circumstances, crises and challenges.

At the heart of the Group's conceptual framework is the question of whether current well-being can be sustained over time without compromising the living conditions and development opportunities of future generations. To this end, the framework considers not only economic and financial resources, but also the five types of capital that underpin a society's well-being and long-term development: produced capital, human capital, social capital, institutional capital and natural capital. Protecting the climate, ecosystems and biodiversity, using natural resources responsibly, and investing in education, health, social cohesion and effective institutions are all important elements in assessing sustainable development. Resilience also encompasses the ability to identify risks at an early stage, respond appropriately to crises and successfully implement the necessary adaptation measures. Societal progress should therefore be assessed not only in terms of present living conditions, but also according to whether the foundations for long-term prosperity, stability and well-being can be preserved and strengthened.
